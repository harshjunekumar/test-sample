# GREAT5 – how this case study shows PepsiCo's leadership principles

PepsiCo's **GREAT5** leadership model (used in interviews): **G**rowth, **R**elationships, **E**xecution, **A**gility, **T**hinking.
Interviewers ask for *real examples* of each – this case gives you five ready-made ones. Use the STAR format
(Situation, Task, Action, Result) and keep each answer to ~1 minute.

| Principle | What PepsiCo means | Evidence in this case | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Growth** | Curious, learns from new situations | Turned EDA patterns into 4 testable hypotheses; openly lists data limits and what I'd learn next (panel data, price elasticity) | Slides 5 and 11 (next steps) |
| **Relationships** | Builds trust across boundaries | Acadia plan co-built with the retailer's category & digital teams; every action has an owner across Brand, E-com, RGM, Customer team | Slides 10–11 |
| **Execution** | Energy; simplifies complexity; quality results | 1m rows → 3 messages; every action has £, lever, KPI, owner, timing; workbook fully traceable (formulas) | Slides 2, 7, 11; workbook |
| **Agility** | Adapts to change, manages ambiguity | Messy, sanitised data → six documented decisions; no buyer counts → stated proxies (CSN basket penetration, share) | Slides 2–3 |
| **Thinking** | External insight, long-term holistic view | Caught a misleading −10% naive comparison (seasonality); shopper/basket view, not just sales; long-term adjacency option (Crackers) | Slides 2, 4, 8 |

## STAR stories to rehearse

**Thinking – "Tell me about a time you challenged an obvious conclusion."**
- *S:* Two years of weekly sales; 2025 data ended at week 47.
- *T:* Measure market growth correctly.
- *A:* Plotted weekly sales, saw the Christmas peak in weeks 48–52 and a moving Easter; compared weeks 1–47 vs 1–47.
- *R:* Avoided a false −10% decline; true growth +1.2% – the base for every conclusion.

**Agility – "How do you handle imperfect data?"**
- *S:* 1m sanitised rows: duplicates, 17% zero rows, odd segment names, no channel or buyer fields.
- *T:* Make it usable without over-cleaning.
- *A:* Profiled each issue, sized its £ impact, made six explicit decisions and listed them as assumptions.
- *R:* A clean, defensible base; the interviewer can see every choice.

**Execution – "How do you make complex analysis actionable?"**
- *A:* Summarised to 8.5k rows, built a formula-driven workbook, sized £ headroom per gap, scored actions on impact / ease / fit.
- *R:* Six prioritised actions, each with owner, KPI and timing; top one = Potato Crisps sharing (£42.9m headroom).

**Relationships – "How would you work with a customer like Acadia?"**
- *A:* Share the fact base (PepsiCo 24.8% vs 38.6% at other online retailers; Acadia shoppers over-index on nuts & better-for-you) as a *joint* growth story, not a PepsiCo ask; co-design an 8-week pilot with a control.
- *R:* Win-win: Acadia grows the category, PepsiCo closes ~£4.2m headroom.

**Growth – "What did you learn / what would you do next?"**
- Learned that PepsiCo's gap is in *where shoppers are moving* (sharing, nuts, adjacencies), not its core.
- Next: validate with shopper panel data (true penetration & AWOP), test sharing price-pack, measure the pilot.

## Tips
- Name the principle lightly ("that's where I tried to think long-term…") – don't over-label.
- Every speaker note in the deck ends with a `GREAT5:` tag to remind you which principle that slide shows.
