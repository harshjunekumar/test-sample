## Part 1 – Business basics (no prior knowledge needed)

**PepsiCo** sells drinks (Pepsi) and snacks. In the UK snacks business it is the market leader (in this data: 36.9% of sales).

**The category:** *Savoury Snacks* = crisps, tortilla chips, nuts, popcorn, crackers etc.

**How the category is organised (from broad to detailed):**
```
Category  →  Segment  →  Sub-segment     →  Brand        →  Product (SKU)
Savoury      Crisps      Potato Crisps      CoastalVale     "Salt & Vinegar 6-pack"
```
I analysed at **sub-segment** level (e.g. Potato Crisps, Nuts & Seeds, Crackers) because the segment names in the data are messy.

**Pack formats (how the product is sold):**

| Pack | What it is | Typical occasion |
|---|---|---|
| Single / Grab / Standard | One small bag | Impulse, lunch, on-the-go |
| Multipack (small / medium / large) | Several small bags in one pack (e.g. 6 x 25g) | Lunchboxes, family weekly shop |
| Sharing | One big bag (e.g. 150g) | Sharing at home, parties, TV night |

**Retailers (customers):** the shops PepsiCo sells through – Escora Retail, Saintbury Stores, Morees Markets, Asdaria Supermarkets, Acadia Grocers.

**Store formats / channels:**

| Format in data | Meaning | My channel |
|---|---|---|
| LARGE FORMAT | Big supermarket | In-store |
| SMALL FORMAT | Convenience store | In-store |
| E-COMMERCE | A supermarket's website (e.g. Escora.com) | Online |
| PURE PLAY | Online-only retailer (no shops) – **Acadia Grocers** | Online |

**Supplier vs brand vs private label:** a *supplier* is the manufacturer (PepsiCo, Valley Trail Foods…). A *brand* is the name on the pack (CoastalVale). *Private label* = the retailer's own brand.

**Why the brief cares about e-commerce:** online shopping is growing, baskets are bigger and planned, and there is no physical shelf – so winning online needs different tactics (search ranking, bundles, multipacks).

## Part 2 – The metrics, explained with tiny examples

| Metric | Formula | Example | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value sales** | £ through the till | £1,077m | Size |
| **Units** | Number of items sold | – | A 6-pack = 1 unit |
| **Price per unit** | Value ÷ Units | £1.82 | Price level |
| **Growth %** | This year ÷ last year − 1 | 1,077 ÷ 1,065 − 1 = **+1.2%** | Momentum |
| **Share** | PepsiCo £ ÷ market £ | 397.6 ÷ 1,077.4 = **36.9%** | Strength vs competitors |
| **Share change (pts)** | Share TY − share YA | 36.9% − 37.8% = **−0.9 pts** | Winning or losing. Use *points*, not % |
| **Mix** | Item £ ÷ total £ | Online 183.6 ÷ 1,077.4 = **17%** | Importance |
| **Contribution to growth** | Item £ change ÷ total £ change | Online +£10.1m ÷ total +£12.4m = **82%** | Who drives growth |
| **Index** | A ÷ B × 100 | 52% ÷ 41% × 100 = **127** | 100 = same; >100 = more; <100 = less |
| **Online index** | Item's share of online sales ÷ its share of in-store sales × 100 | Large multipack 6.9% ÷ 3.0% = **228** | Sells 2.3x more (relatively) online |
| **Fair share index** | PepsiCo share in a pack ÷ PepsiCo share of that whole sub-segment × 100 | 20.1% ÷ 46.4% = **43** | <80 = PepsiCo is under-represented = **gap** |
| **£ headroom** | (Fair share − actual share) × cell sales | (46.4% − 20.1%) × £162.9m = **£42.9m** | Size of the prize (not a forecast) |
| **Penetration** | Buyers ÷ all shoppers | 40 of 100 shoppers buy → 40% | How MANY buy |
| **AWOP** (Average Weight of Purchase) | Sales ÷ buyers = frequency × spend per trip | 8 trips × £2.50 = £20 per buyer | How MUCH each buyer spends |
| **Basket penetration** | Baskets containing item ÷ all baskets | 2,450 ÷ 5,000 = **49%** | Share of trips that include it |
| **Co-purchase %** | % of buyers of A who also bought B | 80% of nuts buyers bought sharing crisps | Bundle potential |

**The one formula to remember:**
```
Sales = Shoppers × Penetration × AWOP          (AWOP = Frequency × Spend per trip)
```
Every recommendation grows either **penetration** (more people buy) or **AWOP** (each buyer spends more).

**Why "pts" not "%" for share change:** share going from 37.8% to 36.9% is −0.9 *points*. Saying "−2.4%" (relative change) confuses leaders.

## Part 5 – How the analysis was done, step by step (as YOU would explain it)

### Step 1 – Open and convert the file
The file came as `.xlsb` (binary Excel). Saved as `.xlsx` (Excel: *File → Save As → Excel Workbook*). Checked row counts and totals matched.

### Step 2 – Check the data quality (EDA)

| Check | Found | Did |
|---|---|---|
| Missing values | Flavour 7,385; Number of packs 3,186 | Kept – not needed |
| Duplicates | 1,600 rows worth £700 | Kept – no impact |
| Zero sales | 17% of rows = £0 | Excluded only from price per unit |
| Odd store format | 'Unallocated' £5.5k | Removed |
| Messy segments | 'Private Label' used as a segment | Used sub-segment |
| No channel column | – | Created Online / In-store |

*In Excel:* filters on each column (*Data → Filter*), `COUNTBLANK`, *Remove Duplicates* preview, pivot of value by store format.

### Step 3 – Choose the comparison period (key decision)
2025 data stops at **week 47**. Weeks 48–52 are Christmas (week 51 ≈ £33m vs ~£23m normal).

- Wrong: 2025 (47 weeks) vs 2024 (52 weeks) = **−10.3%** (fake decline)
- Right: **2025 wk 1–47 (TY) vs 2024 wk 1–47 (YA) = +1.2%**

*In Excel:* add a column `Period = IF(WEEK<=47, IF(YEAR=2025,"TY","YA"), "Exclude")`.

### Step 4 – Add the channel
`Channel = IF(OR(Format="E-COMMERCE",Format="PURE PLAY"),"Online","In-store")`

### Step 5 – Summarise 1 million rows (PivotTable)
*Insert → PivotTable* → Rows: Period, Customer, Store format, Channel, Supplier, Brand, Segment, Sub segment, Pack, Sub pack → Values: Sum of Value, Sum of Units → *Design → Report Layout → Tabular, Repeat all labels, no subtotals* → copy as values into the `Data` tab (8,491 rows). Totals still reconcile (TY £1,077.4m, YA £1,065.0m).

### Step 6 – Build each analysis table with SUMIFS
Example – PepsiCo sales in Potato Crisps this year:
```
=SUMIFS(Data!K:K, Data!A:A,"TY", Data!H:H,"POTATO CRISPS", Data!E:E,"PepsiCo")
```
Then: Growth `=TY/YA-1` · Share `=PepsiCo/Market` · Share change `=(ShareTY-ShareYA)*100` · Contribution `=(TY-YA)/(TotalTY-TotalYA)`.

### Step 7 – The analyses (what each tab answers)

| Workbook tab | Question | Headline result |
|---|---|---|
| 1_Market | How is the market and PepsiCo doing, by channel and retailer? | Market +1.2%; online +5.8% = 82% of growth; PepsiCo −0.9 pts |
| 2_SubSegment | Where does growth come from? | Potato Crisps −£12.9m; growth in Crackers, Bread Snacks, Nuts (PepsiCo 0–7%) |
| 4_PackFormat | Which packs grow, where is PepsiCo weak, what sells online? | Sharing 38% of market, PepsiCo 25.6%; multipacks over-trade online |
| 5_Whitespace | Which sub-segment × pack is PepsiCo's biggest gap? | Potato Crisps Sharing: £162.9m, PepsiCo 20.1% vs 46.4% → £42.9m |
| 6_Online_Gaps | Is PepsiCo weaker online than in-store? | £2.5m (Tortilla Sharing, Nuts) |
| 7_Acadia | What's different at Acadia? | PepsiCo 24.8% vs 38.6% elsewhere online → £4.2m |
| 8_Shopper_Basket | How are online shoppers and baskets different? | Families, main shop, 62% baskets > £60, CSN 49% vs 23% |
| 9_CrossBuy | What is bought together? | 80% of nuts buyers buy sharing crisps |
| 10_Opportunities | What first? | Score = impact 50% + ease 30% + fit 20% |

### Step 8 – Size the gaps (worked example)
Potato Crisps Sharing: market £162.9m; PepsiCo share 20.1%; PepsiCo's share of all Potato Crisps 46.4%.

- Fair share index = 20.1 ÷ 46.4 × 100 = **43** (< 80 → gap)
- Headroom = (46.4% − 20.1%) × £162.9m = 26.3% × £162.9m = **£42.9m**
- Say: *"This is headroom if PepsiCo performed in sharing as it does in crisps overall – not a forecast."*

### Step 9 – Prioritise
Each action scored 1–5 on **Impact**, **Ease**, **Fit** → `Score = 0.5×Impact + 0.3×Ease + 0.2×Fit`
Example: Potato Crisps Sharing = 0.5×5 + 0.3×3 + 0.2×5 = **4.4 → High**.

## Part 8 – Interview questions with model answers

**About the method**

1. *Why weeks 1–47?* – 2025 ends at week 47; including Christmas weeks only in 2024 shows a fake −10% decline. Like-for-like gives +1.2%.
2. *How did you measure penetration without buyer data?* – I couldn't directly. I used value share and growth as proxies, plus CSN basket penetration and demographics. I'd validate with a shopper panel (e.g. Kantar).
3. *What's AWOP?* – Average spend per buyer over a period = frequency × spend per trip.
4. *Why sub-segment, not segment?* – Segments are inconsistent ('Private Label' is a segment; Snack Mix sits in two).
5. *How did you handle 1 million rows?* – PivotTable to 8,491 rows, then SUMIFS tables. Every number traceable.
6. *Is the £42.9m real money?* – It's headroom to fair share over 47 weeks, not a forecast. Realistic capture is a fraction – e.g. closing a quarter of the gap ≈ £10m.
7. *Why fair share vs PepsiCo's own sub-segment share?* – It compares PepsiCo to itself, so it isolates *pack* gaps, not categories PepsiCo doesn't play in.

**About the findings**

8. *Biggest single insight?* – PepsiCo is under-represented where growth is: sharing packs, nuts, adjacencies and the fastest-growing online retailer.
9. *Why is PepsiCo weak in sharing?* – Two hypotheses: range (fewer sharing SKUs) and price (18% premium). Next step: test price-pack architecture.
10. *Why online for AWOP?* – Online is the planned weekly shop: bigger baskets (62% > £60), families, and nuts/snacks in 49% of baskets with 1.5x spend.
11. *Why Acadia if it's only £30m?* – It's growing 15.5% and PepsiCo is weakest there; it's also a good test-and-learn partner before scaling online.
12. *Why not recommend entering Crackers first?* – PepsiCo has no brand there; high cost and risk. Start where existing brands can win quickly; build the crackers case in parallel.

**Challenges (stay calm, agree then add)**

13. *"Isn't the data sanitised – can we trust it?"* – Yes, so I focus on relative gaps and direction, which are robust.
14. *"Your scores are subjective."* – They are judgement inputs; the workbook lets you change weights and see the ranking update.
15. *"Couldn't the cross-buy just be coincidence?"* – Possibly; without a base rate I can't compute affinity. A pilot with a control group answers that.
16. *"What would you do with more time/data?"* – Panel data for true penetration/AWOP, price elasticity, promo data, Acadia search-ranking data.
17. *"How would you measure success?"* – PepsiCo share in Potato Crisps Sharing, online AWOP (£ per basket), Acadia share, new-buyer count.
18. *"Which stakeholders do you need?"* – Brand (range), Revenue Management (price-pack), E-com team (search, bundles), Acadia customer team.
