# Maruti Suzuki Victoris VXi AT (Petrol, Non-Strong-Hybrid) vs Midsize-SUV Rivals with Petrol Automatic

**Primary candidate:** Maruti Suzuki Victoris **VXi 1.5 6AT** — 1.5L naturally-aspirated petrol, 6-speed torque-converter automatic, FWD
**Comparison set:** Midsize / C-segment SUVs offered with a **petrol automatic that is not a strong hybrid**
**Research date:** 7 September 2026
**Prices:** ex-showroom Delhi unless stated

---

## 1. Scope, method and one important clarification

### 1.1 The "non-hybrid" label needs a caveat

The Victoris 1.5 petrol **is not a zero-electrification engine**. The K15C DualJet 1462cc unit carries Maruti's **12V SHVS "Smart Hybrid"** system — an integrated starter-generator (ISG) with torque assist, idle stop-start and brake energy regeneration. Every listing site labels this variant "Smart Hybrid petrol".

What the user almost certainly means by "non-hybrid" — and what this document uses — is **"not the strong hybrid"**: the Victoris also offers a separate Toyota-derived 1490cc M15D-FXE three-cylinder **strong hybrid** with an e-CVT and an electric drive motor, claiming 28.65 kmpl. That is a fundamentally different car and is **excluded** from the head-to-head.

So throughout this document:

| Term used here | Means |
|---|---|
| **Non-hybrid / mild-hybrid petrol AT** | 1.5 NA petrol + 12V ISG assist + conventional 6AT (the VXi AT) |
| **Strong hybrid** | Separate engine, e-CVT, electric-only driving — **excluded from comparison** |

The same distinction applies to the Grand Vitara and Hyryder, which are mechanical siblings and also sell both a 12V mild-hybrid NA petrol AT and a strong hybrid.

### 1.2 Method and data-access limitation

**This session's network egress was restricted to search only.** Direct page fetches to CarWale, CarDekho, Team-BHP, Autocar India, ZigWheels, V3Cars, Wikipedia and marutisuzuki.com were all blocked by the environment's egress proxy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT). Every figure below therefore comes from **live web-search retrieval of those same sources' indexed content**, cross-checked across multiple sites where possible, rather than from reading each spec table directly.

Practical consequence: **headline specs (engine, dimensions, tyres, ARAI mileage, prices) are well corroborated and reliable. A handful of secondary figures conflict between sources and are explicitly flagged in §9.** Anything marked ⚠️ should be confirmed at a dealership before you sign.

### Reading the table

- **The Victoris is the least powerful car here** at 102–103 bhp. The Elevate leads on both power (121 bhp) and torque (145 Nm); Creta/Seltos sit at ~115 bhp; the 1.0 TSI twins deliver the most low-end torque of the group thanks to turbocharging.
- **The Victoris's gearbox is its strongest hardware advantage.** It, the GV, Hyryder, Sierra and the VW/Skoda pair use **torque converters**; Creta, Seltos and Elevate all use **CVTs**. Owner reviews consistently flag the CVT "rubber-band effect" under hard acceleration on the Korean and Honda cars — a torque converter avoids that and is generally regarded as the more durable, more linear-feeling automatic in Indian stop-go traffic.
- **The DSG concern does not apply to the 1.0 TSI AT.** VW/Skoda's 1.0-litre now uses an 8-speed conventional torque-converter automatic (replacing the older 6AT); the dry-clutch DSG reliability complaints in owner forums attach to the **1.5 TSI DSG**, a different powertrain.

### Reading the table

- **Ground clearance is a genuine Victoris win.** 210 mm beats the Creta (190), Seltos (~190) and comfortably beats the Taigun/Kushaq (188). Only the Elevate (220) is higher. For rough roads, unmade approaches and tall speed breakers this is a real, felt difference — and it is one of the most frequently praised items in Maruti owner reviews.
- **Boot: the Victoris is the pick of the Suzuki-platform trio by a wide margin.** 439 L vs 373 L for the Grand Vitara and Hyryder — a **66-litre advantage over its own siblings** on an identical 2,600 mm wheelbase. That is arguably the single strongest practical reason to choose Victoris over Grand Vitara.
- **The Seltos is now the size king.** At 4,460 mm long with a 2,690 mm wheelbase, the new-generation Seltos is 100 mm longer and 90 mm longer in wheelbase than the Victoris; expect noticeably better rear-seat legroom. The Sierra's 2,730 mm wheelbase and 622-litre boot are class-leading outright.
- **Cabin width:** Victoris (1,795 mm) is mid-pack. Seltos (1,830) and Sierra (1,841) are meaningfully wider for three-abreast rear seating; the Taigun/Kushaq (1,760) are the narrowest.

### Reading the table

- **The Victoris standardises 215/60 R17 on every single variant** — including the ₹10.49 lakh LXi. That is unusual and genuinely buyer-friendly: no rolling-radius change across the range, one tyre size to shop for, and the base cars are not stuck on small 16-inch rubber the way the Creta, Seltos, Elevate, Taigun and Kushaq base/mid trims are.
- **Practical upshot for the VXi AT:** you get the *full-size* 17-inch tyre of the top trims but on a **steel rim with a plastic cover**. Mechanically identical footprint, contact patch and ride to the ZXi+; purely a cosmetic and unsprung-weight difference. Buyers who want the look can retrofit alloys and keep the same 215/60 R17 tyres.
- **Ride-comfort note:** 215/60 R17 gives a taller, more cushioned sidewall than the Creta/Seltos top trims' 215/55 R18. Combined with 210 mm of clearance this is consistent with the "plush ride over broken roads" verdict in the expert reviews.
- ⚠️ **Spare wheel:** Team-BHP's review flags that the Victoris **does not include a spare tyre as standard** (the wheel well exists for one). Confirm what your specific VXi AT ships with — this is an easily missed omission and a meaningful one on Indian highways.

| Tata Sierra NA petrol AT | ~16–18 kmpl | — (too new) | — | — |

### Reading the table — this is the headline finding

**The Victoris VXi AT is the most fuel-efficient petrol automatic in this comparison, on both claimed and tested numbers, and it is not close.**

- Tested overall **16.10 kmpl** vs Creta 13.33, Seltos 12.26, Taigun 10.45. That is a **~21% advantage over the Creta** and **~31% over the Seltos** — the two cars most buyers actually cross-shop.
- On the highway the gap is starker still: **20.24 kmpl vs 15.05 (Creta) and 14.22 (Seltos)**.
- Over 15,000 km/year at ~₹105/litre, Victoris-vs-Seltos alone is roughly **₹32,000/year** in fuel. Over five years that comfortably exceeds any price premium in the group.
- The 12V mild-hybrid ISG (idle stop-start + regen + torque assist) is doing real work here, and the torque converter is not penalising economy the way the CVTs are in city traffic.

**Two important cautions:**

1. **Avoid the AllGrip AWD if economy matters.** Tested at 11.78 kmpl overall vs 16.10 for the FWD — a **27% penalty**. The VXi AT is FWD, so this does not affect the primary candidate, but it should steer anyone tempted upmarket.
2. **Every car here misses its ARAI claim by 24–48%.** Discount all brochure figures. The Taigun/Kushaq gap (−48%) is the most severe in the group and is corroborated across both tested and owner data — small turbo engines test well and drink in the real world.
3. ⚠️ Some owner reviews report Victoris **strong hybrid** owners getting only 18–19.5 kmpl against a 28+ kmpl claim. That is the strong hybrid, not the VXi AT, but it is a useful signal that Maruti's hybrid claims in particular are optimistic.

## 10. Verdict

### Where the Victoris VXi AT wins

1. **Fuel efficiency — decisively.** 16.10 kmpl tested overall against 13.33 (Creta), 12.26 (Seltos), 10.45 (Taigun). Roughly ₹30,000+/year saved versus the Korean CVTs at typical usage.
2. **Safety — uniquely.** The only car in the group with **5 stars from both Bharat NCAP and Global NCAP**, with 6 airbags standard from the base variant.
3. **Gearbox choice.** A torque converter instead of a CVT — no rubber-band effect, better long-term durability reputation, better traffic manners.
4. **Ground clearance (210 mm) and boot (439 L).** Beats both Suzuki-platform siblings on boot by 66 L, and beats Creta/Seltos/Taigun on clearance.
5. **Tyre standardisation.** 215/60 R17 on every variant including the base — nobody else does this.
6. **Running costs.** Maruti's service network and parts pricing remain the segment benchmark; Grand Vitara owners report ₹5,000–7,000 routine services.

### Where it loses

1. **Power — it is the slowest car here.** 102–103 bhp vs 121 (Elevate) and 115 (Creta/Seltos). Owners and reviewers agree it feels lethargic for highway overtakes. If you drive long highway stretches with a full car, drive it before buying.
2. **No turbo-petrol, no diesel.** If you want performance, the Victoris has no answer at all.
3. **VXi trim is visually and experientially basic.** Steel wheels, halogen lights, small display. The ₹1.77 lakh jump to ZXi AT buys a lot of perceived quality.
4. **Road and tyre noise** at speed, per multiple reviews.
5. **Size vs the new Seltos.** 100 mm shorter, 90 mm less wheelbase, 35 mm narrower — rear-seat space is a real Seltos advantage.
6. **Missing convenience kit:** spare tyre, rain-sensing wipers, LED cabin lights, cooled glovebox.

### Recommendation matrix

| If your priority is… | Buy |
|---|---|
| **Running cost, efficiency, safety, low-fuss ownership** | **Victoris VXi AT** — the rational pick, and the point of this comparison |
| Feature-per-rupee at the same money | **Victoris ZXi AT** (₹15.13 L) — the VXi's gaps are exactly what ZXi fills |
| Maximum rear-seat space | Kia Seltos (2026) or Tata Sierra |
| Outright performance / engine punch | Honda Elevate (121 bhp) or VW Taigun / Skoda Kushaq (turbo torque) |
| Driving enjoyment and handling | VW Taigun / Skoda Kushaq — but accept ~10.5 kmpl and a firm ride |
| Biggest boot | Tata Sierra (622 L), then Honda Elevate (458 L), then Victoris (439 L) |
| Brand cachet and feature theatre | Hyundai Creta or Kia Seltos — pay for it in fuel |
| **Avoid if efficiency matters** | Victoris **AllGrip AWD AT** (11.78 kmpl tested) and Taigun/Kushaq 1.0 TSI AT (10.45 kmpl) |

**Bottom line:** on the specific brief — *petrol, automatic, non-strong-hybrid, this segment* — the Victoris VXi AT is the strongest overall package, and it wins on the two things that cost money over a decade of ownership: fuel and safety. Its one genuine weakness is engine performance, and that weakness is real rather than cosmetic. The honest recommendation is to test-drive it fully loaded on a highway before committing, and to seriously price the ZXi AT, because the VXi's omissions are concentrated in exactly the areas owners notice daily.
