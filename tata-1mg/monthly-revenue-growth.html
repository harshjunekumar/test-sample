Where the revenue actually is

01 — Diagnosis

Monthly revenue at a health platform is an identity, not a mystery. For each line of business it is monthly transacting users × orders per user × average order value × net realisation. Four numbers. Every strategy is a claim about which of them moves.

The instinct is to push the first term — more users, more installs, more coupons. At a pharmacy AOV near ₹1,180 and a blended product margin in the low twenties, a discount-acquired user needs roughly five orders to pay back. Most never get there. Meanwhile the second and fourth terms are leaking against users already acquired.

Monthly revenue, ₹190 Cr baseline

Pharmacy carries the volume; diagnostics and wellness carry the margin. Ancillary — retail media, teleconsult, offline and B2B — is the only line with no inventory behind it.

₹0₹190 Cr / month

Three structural facts follow from that split, and they set the whole plan:

01 Chronic is an annuity that leaks

A patient on metformin, glimepiride and a statin should generate twelve refill orders a year. Platforms typically capture seven or eight. A missed month isn't deferred revenue — the patient walks to the chemist downstairs, and the habit resets.

02 Out-of-stock is silent churn

An unserviceable line at a pin code doesn't lose its own value alone. It splits the basket: the patient needs all three strips together, so the whole order goes offline. Line fill rate is a revenue KPI wearing an ops badge.

03 The molecule already names the test

Metformin implies HbA1c every ninety days. Thyroxine implies TSH. Pharmacy knows the molecule and the dose date; diagnostics carries roughly twice the gross margin. Single-digit cross-penetration is the largest un-priced asset in the business.

The thesis in one line: treat the chronic patient-month — not the order — as the unit of revenue. Every lever below either adds a patient-month, protects one that was going to leak, or raises what a patient-month is worth.

The footprint: who is in the system, and what they are hired to do

02 — Actors

Revenue is produced by a chain of actors, and it stops at the weakest one. A verifying pharmacist with a nine-minute queue caps the growth of every demand lever above them. Each card below states what the actor is there for, the job they hire the platform to do, and the metric that says whether they got it.

Levers 1, 3 and 4 double-count: a Care Plan member who is also on auto-refill is one patient, counted in two models. Netting the overlap at roughly 10% puts the honest number at ₹38–40 Cr a month, or about +20%. Say that to the board before someone else finds it.

And what not to do. Three things will be proposed in the same meeting and should lose. Deeper platform-wide discounting — payback runs past three quarters at this AOV, and it trains the base to wait for the coupon. New-city expansion before line fill in the existing top twenty is above 95% — you would be buying demand you cannot serve, which costs the order and the customer. A new vertical before the chronic engine works: every launch competes for the same product and pharmacist capacity that levers 1, 2 and 6 need.

Sequencing

05 — Execution

The first thirty days buy no revenue. They buy the instrumentation without which the other eleven months are guesswork — specifically, a dose-days model per user and a line fill rate cut by pin code.

Days 0–30

Instrument, then ship the cheapest lever

  • Dose-days model per user per molecule, from pack size, strength and directions — the spine of levers 1, 2 and 3.
  • Line fill rate reported by pin code and by chronic molecule, daily, owned by growth as well as ops.
  • Chronic cohort definition agreed once, across pharmacy and labs, so the cross-sell in month three has a denominator.
  • Ship refill reminders on the top 12 chronic molecules. Measure reminder→order conversion by day offset.
Days 31–60

Protect the basket, then price the habit

  • Standing orders (auto-refill) with pause and skip, plus a pre-dispatch confirmation so nothing ships unwanted.
  • Substitution at cart for out-of-stock lines — equivalent brand, pharmacist-approved, price delta shown.
  • Care Plan repriced around the chronic basket; sold at the second refill, not at signup.
  • Attach engine live on the top condition–OTC pairs, capped at two suggestions per cart.
Days 61–90

Open the high-margin adjacencies

  • Protocol-based lab prompts to the chronic cohort, timed to the refill, with slot booking in the same flow.
  • Retail media beta with 6–8 brands on molecule search; compliance review gate on every creative.
  • Discount depth by cohort replaces the flat rate; hold a control group so the margin gain is provable.
Quarters 2–4

Scale what the first quarter proved

  • Extend auto-refill beyond the top 12 molecules; push refill-on-time from the low fifties toward 58%.
  • Omnichannel: stores and collection centres as fulfilment nodes for same-day, not just as retail.
  • Corporate and insurer channel — priced per covered life, activation owned as a KPI, not left to HR mailers.
  • Private label depth in the top five OTC categories, where the attach engine has already proven demand.

Guardrails — the metrics that must not move

A frequency push can be faked with discounts, and an attach engine can be faked with pressure. These are the counter-metrics that tell you the growth is real, and two of them are non-negotiable.

On the numbers. The baseline of ₹190 Cr a month and every L2/L3 value in the tree are illustrative — a consistent model built to make the structure legible and the arithmetic checkable, not a report of Tata 1mg's actual figures, which are not public at this granularity. Replace them with actuals and the tree, the levers and the sequencing all still hold; only the sizing moves. Cross-checks worth running first: orders/MTU against your own chronic cohort, line fill rate at the line level rather than the order level, and net realisation after returns rather than after discount alone.
