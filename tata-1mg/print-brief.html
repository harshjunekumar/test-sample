01Chronic is an annuity that leaks

A patient on metformin, glimepiride and a statin should generate twelve refills a year. Platforms capture seven or eight. A missed month isn't deferred — the patient walks to the chemist downstairs and the habit resets.

02Out-of-stock is silent churn

An unserviceable line doesn't lose its own value alone. It splits the basket: the patient needs all three strips together, so the whole order goes offline. Line fill rate is a revenue KPI wearing an ops badge.

03The molecule already names the test

Metformin implies HbA1c every ninety days; thyroxine implies TSH. Pharmacy knows the molecule and the dose date, and diagnostics carries twice the margin. Single-digit cross-penetration is the largest un-priced asset here.

The thesis: treat the chronic patient-month — not the order — as the unit of revenue. Every lever either adds a patient-month, protects one that was going to leak, or raises what a patient-month is worth.

And what should lose the same meeting. Deeper platform-wide discounting — payback runs past three quarters at this AOV and it trains the base to wait for the coupon. New-city expansion before line fill in the existing top twenty clears 95% — buying demand you cannot serve costs the order and the customer. A new vertical before the chronic engine works: every launch competes for the same product and pharmacist capacity that levers 1, 2 and 6 need.

Sequencing

The first thirty days buy no revenue. They buy the instrumentation without which the other eleven months are guesswork.

Days 0–30

Instrument, ship the cheapest lever

  • Dose-days model per user per molecule — the spine of levers 1, 2 and 3
  • Line fill rate by pin code, daily, owned by growth as well as ops
  • One chronic cohort definition shared by pharmacy and labs
  • Refill reminders on the top 12 molecules
Days 31–60

Protect the basket, price the habit

  • Standing orders with pause, skip and pre-dispatch confirmation
  • Substitution at cart for short lines — equivalent brand, pharmacist-approved
  • Care Plan repriced around the chronic basket, sold at the second refill
  • Attach engine on top condition–OTC pairs, two suggestions maximum
Days 61–90

Open the high-margin adjacencies

  • Protocol lab prompts to the chronic cohort, timed to the refill, booking in the same flow
  • Retail media beta with 6–8 brands, compliance gate on creative
  • Discount depth by cohort replaces the flat rate, with a held-out control
Quarters 2–4

Scale what the first quarter proved

  • Auto-refill beyond the top 12; refill-on-time toward 58%
  • Omnichannel: stores and collection centres as same-day fulfilment nodes
  • Corporate and insurer channel, priced per covered life, activation owned
  • Private label depth where attach already proved demand
